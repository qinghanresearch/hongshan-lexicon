Civilization and social complexity

文明

Choose wording by the task of the sentence: historical narrative, social analysis, criteria, or official formulation.

What is civilization doing in the sentence?

文明 may name a historical formation, characterize social organization, or invoke value and progress. Rendering every occurrence as civilization can suggest identical criteria throughout. Replacing every occurrence with complex society can remove an argument the author deliberately makes.

First identify the task: a history of civilization, analysis of social complexity, debate over criteria, or reproduction of an official formulation.

Social complexity is an analytical question

Settlement scale and hierarchy, specialization, ritual organization, and differences in authority provide possible dimensions of analysis. Each requires evidence and a method. A particular jade object or large building does not establish a complete institutional system.

Complex society names a social formation; social complexity names a dimension or degree of organization. They have different grammatical roles, and neither replaces every meaning of 文明.

Preserve the author's framework

The era of archaic states poses a related problem: archaic state, proto-state, and chiefdom carry different assumptions. Explain the author's criteria rather than silently substituting another classification. Criticism belongs in an attributed argument, not an invisible translation change.

The later Su dossier favors ancient in “ancient cultures, ancient cities, and ancient states”; the earlier entry records archaic states and proto-state era. Their relationship awaits reconciliation. No dictionary-wide substitution has been made.

Translation and usage

Context Direction Qualification
Histories and origins of civilizations civilization Preserve the author's framework
Social organization complex society / social complexity Formation versus analytical dimension
Debates over thresholds criteria for defining civilization Identify the criteria and proponent
Historical processes the process of civilization Avoid casually introducing a specialized theoretical term
Official documents and fixed quotations The official translation Preserve the version quoted

Precision requires more than length

A useful explanation identifies who uses a concept, which evidence is involved, which criteria matter, what alternatives are considered, and how strong the conclusion is. Fluent prose cannot replace a missing citation.

This lexicon explains translation choices and their consequences. It does not decide through terminology alone whether Hongshan meets a particular definition of civilization.
