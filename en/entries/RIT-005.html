The jade ritual system

More than the use of jade

The jade ritual system refers to an interpretation of patterned jade use as an institution. In the account recorded here, Liu Guoxiang connects the types, forms, and combinations of jades with the social status of the deceased. The word system carries that claim about organized differentiation.

Observation and interpretation

Finding jade in a grave is an observation. Establishing a recurring relationship between assemblages and burial status requires comparison. Interpreting that relationship as a ritual institution is a further step. A clear entry should identify which of these steps the author is making.

The dossier also records comparisons with the jade practices described in the Zhouli. Such comparisons are interpretive arguments involving texts and archaeological material of different dates. They should not be translated as direct evidence that a fully documented later institution already existed unchanged in Hongshan times.

Translation and usage

Use the jade ritual system when conveying the institutional argument. If the source only describes the use of jade, a more limited expression such as “ritual use of jade” may be appropriate. Replacing one with the other changes the strength of the claim.

A useful comparison

Read alongside jade-only burial: the latter characterizes grave goods, while the former proposes a broader relationship between ritual, objects, and social order.
