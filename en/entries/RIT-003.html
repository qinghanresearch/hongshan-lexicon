Stone burial mounds

积石冢

Distinguish a mound from the graves within it, then compare stone burial mound, cairn, barrow, and tumulus.

What is a jishizhong?

A jishizhong is a burial mound characterized by stone construction. Distinguish the mound as a whole from an individual grave or burial within it. Translating both levels simply as tomb can obscure cemetery organization.

This also affects discussions of burial groups, sequence, and hierarchy. The layout of each monument must be established from its excavation record.

Mound, tomb, and burial

Referent Possible wording What to specify
The stone-built mound as a whole stone mound / stone burial mound Material, form, and extent
A tomb with a stone mound stone-mounded tomb Whether the tomb is the sentence's focus
An individual grave or interment tomb / burial Structure, act, or deposited remains

The existing decision favors stone-mounded tomb; its relationship to stone mound tomb remains under review. Stone burial mound is a descriptive option for the whole monument, not an approved dictionary-wide replacement.

Comparing cairn, barrow, and tumulus

Cairn emphasizes stone construction but does not always establish a funerary purpose. Barrow is familiar in British prehistoric archaeology; Historic England includes earth, stone, and mixed constructions. Tumulus is not necessarily earthen either: the Dartmoor survey uses stone tumulus when explaining cairn. [1]–[2]

Preferring an explicit description in a Hongshan text is a translation strategy, not a universal verdict on English terminology.

Translation and usage

Identify the referent before selecting the term. When discussing mounds or several graves within one mound, stone burial mound can make the scale clear. Preserve an established term when quoting a publication, explaining any difference in scope.

Limits of the comparison

Do not turn the possibility of multiple graves into a claim about every mound. These English references clarify terminology; they do not establish the structure of an individual Niuheliang monument. This correction of the earlier blanket exclusion of barrow and tumulus awaits editorial review.
