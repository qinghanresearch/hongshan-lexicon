The “post-Hongshan Culture”

后红山文化

Keep provisional historical naming distinct from later classifications.

The discussion of Su Bingqi here refers to “辽西古文化古城古国——兼谈当前田野考古工作的重点或大课题,” Wenwu 文物, no. 8 (1986), pp. 41–44. The following exposition discusses the concepts and their translation rather than quoting the article verbatim; pinpoint references remain to be checked.

Post-Hongshan is not late Hongshan

The late phase of the Hongshan Culture is an internal chronological division. Post-Hongshan places the referent after Hongshan. Translating both as Later Hongshan obscures that difference.

A provisional name carries information

In the article identified above, Su Bingqi uses the tentative designation “post-Hongshan Culture.” The provisional wording and quotation marks convey the status of the classification; they are not disposable decoration.

The dossier associates this historical label with the subsequently named Xiaoheyan Culture. In a translation of the historical text, explain that correspondence in a note rather than replacing the author's name with the later one.

Translation and usage

Use the “post-Hongshan Culture”, retaining wording such as tentatively named. For an internal late phase, use the late phase of the Hongshan Culture instead.

What still requires checking?

The exact range of material, correspondence with the later name, naming history, and chronology should be checked against primary publications. This edition preserves the distinction without adding an unverified precise timeline.
