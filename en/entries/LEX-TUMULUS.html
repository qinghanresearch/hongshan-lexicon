Tumulus

tumulus：墓冢

A mound term that does not by itself determine construction material or internal structure.

IN THIS ENTRY

What does tumulus denote?

Here tumulus denotes a mound over a burial. The word alone does not specify material, chamber construction, number of interments, or date. It should not automatically be understood as an exclusively earthen structure.

The Dartmoor survey glossary held by Historic England uses stone tumulus when explaining cairn. Stone construction is therefore not incompatible with the word. [1]

Relationship to barrow and cairn

Usage overlaps and depends on regional and classificatory traditions. Consult the author's definition of the monument rather than relying on a one-to-one bilingual list. A local survey convention is not automatically a classification for Chinese burials.

Translation and usage

Chinese equivalents may include 墓冢 or 坟冢, with material specified where needed. When using tumulus for 积石冢, explain its stone construction and distinguish the mound from individual graves. Choosing stone burial mound for accessibility is an editorial preference, not a declaration that tumulus is incorrect.

Questions to ask

Does the author mean the visible mound or a monument including underground structures? Is the word descriptive or classificatory? What evidence establishes material and construction?
