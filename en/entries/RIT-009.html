The era of archaic states

古国时代

A Chinese archaeological framework whose relationship to state, proto-state, and chiefdom requires explanation.

The discussion of Su Bingqi here refers to “辽西古文化古城古国——兼谈当前田野考古工作的重点或大课题,” Wenwu 文物, no. 8 (1986), pp. 41–44. The following exposition discusses the concepts and their translation rather than quoting the article verbatim; pinpoint references remain to be checked.

A concept within Chinese archaeological debate

The era of archaic states renders 古国时代, a periodizing and interpretive concept linked in the editorial dossier to Su Bingqi's sequence of ancient cultures, ancient cities, and ancient states. It should not be assumed to map directly onto any single Anglophone model of political development.

Why state, proto-state, and chiefdom are not interchangeable

Each term carries assumptions about authority, institutions, and social organization. Replacing an author's 古国 with chiefdom may substitute a different analytical framework. Conversely, using state without explaining the author's criteria can suggest a stronger or different claim than intended.

The seed decisions record the era of archaic states and proto-state era. The later Su translation dossier prefers ancient states within its three-part title. These records concern related expressions but do not yet establish a single reconciled wording for every context. This edition keeps the distinction visible pending editorial review.

Translation and usage

Use the era of archaic states for this entry's heading. When discussing another author's terminology, preserve that wording and explain its criteria. Avoid alternating between archaic states, proto-states, and chiefdoms as if the difference were merely stylistic.

A question for the reader

Ask what evidence the author treats as diagnostic: settlement hierarchy, political autonomy, ritual authority, or some combination. A useful translation communicates that reasoning as well as the label.
