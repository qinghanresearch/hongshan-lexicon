The Hongshan cultures

红山诸文化

A plural designation preserves relationships among several units, rather than naming one site complex.

The discussion of Su Bingqi here refers to “辽西古文化古城古国——兼谈当前田野考古工作的重点或大课题,” Wenwu 文物, no. 8 (1986), pp. 41–44. The following exposition discusses the concepts and their translation rather than quoting the article verbatim; pinpoint references remain to be checked.

Why preserve the plural?

The Hongshan cultures renders Su Bingqi's collective designation 红山诸文化 in a particular argument. 诸 preserves a relationship among several cultures or cultural types discussed within a common regional trajectory. It is not simply an alternative name for the singular Hongshan Culture.

What the designation does

In this argument, Su grouped several Liaoxi types in a discussion of shared development. Distinguish the objects included, the evidence connecting them, and the further social interpretation. A collective name does not independently demonstrate the final interpretation.

Why not simply use complex?

Complex can denote assemblages or groups of structures; its classificatory level varies by tradition. The concern is contextual: the Hongshan complex might suggest a site complex or a singular entity where the Chinese deliberately preserves plurality.

Tradition, horizon, and sphere also carry technical associations. They should be used only if those associations fit the author's classification, not merely because they sound more specialized.

Translation and usage

This lexicon uses the Hongshan cultures (Hongshan zhu wenhua 红山诸文化), with an explanation of its scope at first mention. This decision concerns Su's argument; it is not a prohibition on another author's independently defined use of Hongshan complex.

Historical classifications

If a “type” was later recognized as a “culture,” explain the change in a note rather than retroactively altering the historical text. Read this entry alongside the four Hongshan expressions to understand the temporal and regional organization involved.
