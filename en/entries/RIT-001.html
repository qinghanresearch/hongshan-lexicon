Juedi tiantong: severing communication

绝地天通

An ancient account of communication with the divine, its modern interpretation, and the limits of applying texts to prehistoric archaeology.

IN THIS ENTRY

What does the expression mean?

Juedi tiantong describes the severing of communication between Heaven and Earth. It concerns who may mediate between human and divine realms, rather than a physical separation of two places. Narratives associated with the expression occur in the Shangshu, “Lü xing,” and the Guoyu, “Chu yu xia.”

From textual narrative to archaeological interpretation

K. C. Chang drew on the Guoyu account in discussing shamanism and political authority. In that framework, restricted access to supernatural communication becomes part of an explanation of kingship. The account is a source for an interpretation, not a contemporary description of Hongshan society.

The editorial dossier records Jia Xiaobing's further association of this kind of change with the middle Hongshan period. This involves a comparison between later textual traditions and archaeological evidence. Similarity does not by itself establish that the event described in the text occurred at a particular prehistoric site.

Translation and usage

Use severing the communication between Heaven and Earth (juedi tiantong 绝地天通) at first mention. Retain qualifications such as “may reflect” when the author is proposing a connection. “Cutting off heaven and earth” obscures what is being severed: communication.

What the wording must preserve

Identify whose argument is being presented and separate the ancient narrative, the modern theoretical model, and the archaeological application. These are three different levels of evidence.
