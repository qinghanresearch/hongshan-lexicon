The Youxiong lineage

A name with competing explanations

Youxiong shi is a proper name associated with the Yellow Emperor tradition. The editorial dossier records interpretations involving a place or polity name as well as interpretations connecting the name with a bear totem. Translating the name directly as an animal would decide the question before explaining it.

Why retain Youxiong?

The Youxiong lineage preserves the name while offering a provisional rendering of shi. “Lineage” should itself be checked against the passage: it is a translation choice, not archaeological proof of a particular form of kinship organization.

The dossier attributes bear-totem readings to Sun Zuoyun and Ye Shuxian and records Guo Dashun's association between Youxiong and the jade bear-dragon. When discussing that argument, state the attribution. A resemblance between an object and a bear does not independently establish the identity of its makers or users.

Translation and usage

At first mention, use the Youxiong lineage (Youxiong shi 有熊氏). Avoid “Lord of the Bears”: it converts shi into a title and makes the animal interpretation part of the name itself.

Related interpretive problem

The same distinction between description and interpretation matters for the jade pig-dragon / jade bear-dragon. A translation should allow readers to recognize the referent while keeping the argument visible.
