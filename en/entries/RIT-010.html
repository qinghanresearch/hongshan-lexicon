Wu and shaman

A familiar translation that must retain the theoretical framework and debates behind it.

A translation that carries a theory

The correspondence between wu and shaman is disputed. This edition uses shaman when presenting the theoretical framework associated with K. C. Chang, while acknowledging alternatives such as ritualist and medium.

Why the choice matters

Shaman brings a comparative framework to the interpretation of ritual specialists. Medium highlights mediation, while ritualist is broader. They do not necessarily identify the same activities or social roles. Choosing among them requires attention to the author's argument and the evidence being discussed.

The editorial dossier records earlier translations including witch and wizard. These can carry associations that are inappropriate to the passage. In particular, vocabulary associated with witchcraft should not introduce a moral condemnation absent from the Chinese.

Translation and usage

At first mention within the relevant framework, use shaman (wu 巫). Where the author deliberately distinguishes wu from another category, preserve and explain that distinction. Where “巫或萨满” is an explanatory pairing, wu (shamans) may avoid manufacturing a contrast that the author did not intend.

Applying the term to Hongshan

Do not treat an object interpreted as ritual equipment as independent proof that its user was a shaman. The identification belongs to an argument and should be presented with its attribution and degree of certainty.
