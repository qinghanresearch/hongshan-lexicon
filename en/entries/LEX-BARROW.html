A word for a burial mound

In archaeological usage, barrow commonly denotes a burial mound. It is especially familiar from British prehistoric archaeology, but this does not establish an absolute geographical restriction or an earth-only definition. Historic England's introduction includes earth and stone constructions. [1]

Why prefer another expression for jishizhong 积石冢?

Semantic possibility and communicative clarity are different questions. For a general international readership, stone burial mound can explicitly identify the material and funerary context. If an author uses barrow, explain the referent rather than declaring it wrong simply because another label is preferred.

Translation and usage

墓冢 may be suitable in Chinese, with specific types and definitions preserved. For Chinese monuments, consider whether additional material information is needed and whether the wording distinguishes the mound from graves within it.

Comparison with tumulus and cairn

Barrow and tumulus can overlap in reference. Cairn foregrounds a stone-built feature. They are not three universally fixed, mutually exclusive categories applicable unchanged in every region.
