Jade-only burial

唯玉为葬

A description of grave goods

Jade-only burial renders a characterization of Hongshan burial practice in which jade objects are described as the exclusive grave goods. The phrase concerns the assemblage accompanying a burial; it should not be read as saying that the body, coffin, or entire burial structure consisted of jade.

Where the concept enters the argument

The editorial dossier traces the formulation to Mou Yongkang and Wu Ruzuo in 1992 and contrasts it with discussions of jade used in enshrouding the dead at Liangzhu. That attribution still requires checking against the original publication before a final scholarly citation is supplied.

The description also forms part of arguments about a jade ritual system. An observed selection of grave goods and an inference about an institution are different claims: the second requires an argument about patterns, social differentiation, and context.

Translation and usage

Use jade-only burial as a compact term, or burial exclusively with jades in explanatory prose. State the geographical, chronological, and social scope of the observation. Do not extend a description of selected large tombs to every Hongshan burial without evidence.

Reading the evidence

Questions of preservation, excavation, and the definition of “grave goods” matter when assessing exclusivity. The translation should preserve the source author's claim without turning it into an unrestricted statement about the whole culture.
