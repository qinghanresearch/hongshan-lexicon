Jade pig-dragon / jade bear-dragon

Why does one object category have two names?

Jade pig-dragon 玉猪龙 and jade bear-dragon 玉熊龙 embody different readings of a coiled jade form. “Jade” identifies the material; “pig” or “bear” involves interpretation; “dragon” adds conventional naming and meaning. Neither is simply a measurement of shape.

Ask whether the author identifies an object category or argues for an animal image and symbolic significance. The first task needs a recognizable label; the second needs evidence and attribution.

From visible form to symbolic argument

A coiled body, a central opening, and projections at the head provide starting points for description. Identifying an animal is another step; connecting it with a religious or mythological tradition requires a further argument.

The editorial dossier associates the pig reading with earlier naming from head features. It records Guo Dashun's bear interpretation in relation to hibernation and revival, bear-related beliefs in Liaoxi, and the Youxiong 有熊 tradition. These are attributed interpretations, not functions demonstrated by the name.

Choosing the English name

The translation record cites catalogue usage including The Golden Age of Chinese Archaeology (1999), selecting jade pig-dragon for general description. Exact pages and object identifications still need verification before formal citation.

Use jade bear-dragon when discussing Guo Dashun's interpretation, with attribution and “also known as a jade pig-dragon” at first mention. Explain why the name changes rather than implying a different object category.

What an image can establish

A photograph supports observation of form. It cannot independently establish an animal prototype, function, tomb occupant, or mythological connection. The candidate photograph is omitted from the public edition because publication rights remain unresolved.
