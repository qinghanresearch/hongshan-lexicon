The “Goddess Temple”

女神庙

An established site name can be retained while interpretations of the figures and building remain open.

A conventional name, not a settled identification

The “Goddess Temple” is the conventional name of a structure at Niuheliang. The editorial dossier associates its naming with the discovery of clay human figures at Locality 1 in 1983. The name helps readers identify the site, but it also embeds interpretations of the figures and the building.

Two questions within one name

“Goddess” identifies the represented beings in a particular way. “Temple” classifies the structure by function. Neither question is settled simply by repeating the established name. The dossier records interpretations involving goddesses, a mother goddess, ancestral deities, and an ancestral-temple context.

These alternatives should be attributed to their proponents, rather than merged into a single description. A translation can preserve an established site name while explaining that its interpretation remains debated.

Translation and usage

Use the “Goddess Temple” at first mention, accompanied by a short explanation that it is a conventional designation. In a quotation from a particular institutional document, retain that document's wording. The dossier records UNESCO usage, but the precise document should be checked when making a formal citation.

What to look for in a fuller account

A strong account should distinguish the excavated structure, the locations and condition of the figures, and competing reconstructions of ritual activity. Naming alone cannot supply those details.
